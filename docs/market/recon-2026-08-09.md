# Recon run — 2026-08-09 — the upward contract

**Brief:** all five standing questions, discovery depth. Personas in scope:
devtools/platform teams, OSS maintainers, data/research teams, agent-output
owners, and (added by the operator) compliance-driven enterprises needing
byte-level lineage from meeting note through Jira ticket to shipped product.

**Stage context:** pre-validation. No persona library existed, `experiments.toml`
is empty, the discovery landing page is built but has never been driven by
traffic, and no segment has ever been observed. Recon intensity was therefore
kept at first-pass depth per question rather than a full landscape teardown.

**Artifacts written:** `niche-map.md`, `failure-ledger.md`,
`paygate-teardowns.md`, and `personas/*` (alternatives, watering holes,
mistaken-identity sets only — identity is the brain's gate, not recon's).

## 1. A priori feasibility — what research can and cannot buy

Stated before the findings, so nothing downstream gets planned around data that
does not exist:

- **Real revenue, churn, CAC, and retention are private for every competitor
  here.** Nothing in this run indicates whether Mintlify or ReadMe is actually
  growing, or whether anyone renews.
- **List price ≠ realized price.** ReadMe's Pro is annual-billing-only and
  Enterprise is "$3,000+"; both invite negotiation. Every number in the teardowns
  is a ceiling.
- **The compliance market does not publish prices at all.** No public per-user
  price was findable for Jama Connect, Polarion, or codebeamer. That opacity is
  a finding about the *motion* (quoted, procurement-led) and a hard floor on
  what can be known about the *price*.
- **Premium data was out of reach.** Crunchbase/PitchBook/analyst reports are
  paywalled; funding figures below come from press releases and reporting, which
  lag and are written to flatter.
- **Third-party pricing pages are adversarial.** The first search pass returned
  mostly competitor-authored "<vendor> pricing 2026" SEO pages, one of which was
  materially wrong about ReadMe. Primary pages were fetched wherever possible;
  Hex and Deepnote figures remain third-party and lower-confidence.
- **Only public deaths are visible**, which is why the most important finding in
  this run is an *absence* and is reported as ambiguous rather than as good news.

## 2. Observed reality — the findings

Full detail with sources and confidence lives in the three artifact files. The
five that change decisions:

### F1 — The docs category was repriced to $0 two months ago (high confidence)
Mintlify's Starter **and** Pro are $0 base (Pro with unlimited editor seats);
the meter moved to **AI credits** ($0.01/credit over 10,000/mo). This landed on
$45M Series B at a **$500M valuation** (April 2026, a16z + Salesforce Ventures),
~20,000 companies, on the explicit thesis that **docs will be read more by AI
agents than by humans**.

**Direct consequence for `docs/specs/freeform/pricing-strategy.md`:** Pro at
$29/mo gated on "10 private projects, 3 editors" charges for two things a funded
competitor now gives away. The meter that survives this event is execution or
verification, not projects and seats.

### F2 — Nobody sells verification, because nobody sells verification (high confidence, ambiguous meaning)
Across every paid product torn down, **not one gates execution-plus-expectation
checking of a document.** The free OSS tier (doctests, `mdBook test`, Sphinx
doctest, Quarto, Runme) does per-language example testing; the free hosted tier
does rendering; the middle is empty. **This is either whitespace or an empty
room, and desk research cannot tell you which.**

### F3 — The graveyard's lesson is friction, not demand (high confidence on the citations)
- **Literate programming (1984→)** never crossed over. Cited causes: parallel
  maintenance burden, **authors isolated from artifacts they cannot edit
  directly**, weak tooling, weak examples. Class: `execution` + `timing` — and
  Jupyter later reconstituted the same idea at 40M+ monthly downloads by
  removing the friction and never using the term.
- **Distill.pub** went on indefinite hiatus (2021-07) with a real written
  post-mortem: volunteer burnout and doubt about the journal model. **Demand for
  the artifact was never cited as the problem.** Class: `execution` (labor model).
- **Observable** did not die — it walked back cloud-only notebooks twice,
  landing on an open file format and a desktop app (2025-07). Class: `timing`.
  Corroborates the file-in-your-repo thesis *and* announces a competitor on it.
- **Gigantum** was absorbed by Digital Science (2025-04) with **no post-mortem
  found** — cause unknown, not inferred.
- **Netflix Polynote** and **Nextjournal** were checked and are **alive**;
  logged so a later session does not mis-map their niches as vacant.

### F4 — The compliance niche has a statutory clock (high confidence on dates)
EU AI Act Art. 12 logging obligations bite **2026-08-02**; EU CRA reporting and
artifact-provenance obligations **2026-09-11**, full application
**2027-12-11**. The incumbents (Jama/Polarion/codebeamer for traceability,
Vanta/Drata for GRC, SBOM tooling for supply chain) all trace *records humans
maintain* or *components entering a build* — **none derives lineage
mechanically**. Adjacent capital is moving: Norm AI **$120M Series C** (July
2026) for compliance agents selling an audit trail; Code Ocean ($16.5M Series B)
now pitching "reproducible, **compliant**" agentic workflows.

**But:** this cohort is not on HN, not on Reddit, and buys through procurement
and auditors — which contradicts the product's stated self-serve, no-sales-team
motion. The only compatible route is land-and-expand through engineers.

### F5 — The one niche with proven WTP is the one that is already contested (medium confidence)
Notebooks have settled at **$36–$75/editor/month** (Hex, Deepnote). That is the
best-proven willingness to pay found anywhere in this run — and **marimo**
($5M seed, Nov 2024; Jeff Dean and Clem Delangue among the angels) has already
taken the "reactive, reproducible, git-friendly, pure-file" position there.
Hickory's remaining distinction in that niche is expectation-assertions and
provenance: a feature-level difference against a funded incumbent.

Note the pricing implication in passing: Team at $149/mo for 10 editors is
**$14.90/editor**, about a third of the category floor.

## 3. Confusion points — second-hand proxy only

**Recon runs no personas and observed no one.** Everything here is *reported*
confusion, drawn from post-mortems, retrospectives, and public forums. It is
raw material for real persona probes, not evidence.

- **Reported (literate-programming retrospectives):** practitioners abandoned
  the model because both the code and the formatted document are *derived* and
  therefore not directly editable, cutting authors off from the writing they
  had to do. → **Probe:** hand a persona a woven document, ask them to fix a
  typo in the output, and watch what happens. This is the highest-value
  walkthrough available and it targets the product's core mechanism.
- **Reported (Distill Hiatus, 2021):** the editorial team doubted the *journal*
  framing and wondered whether authors should simply self-publish. → **Probe:**
  does a hosted-workspace framing add friction over "it's a file in my repo"?
- **Reported (Observable forum, "Where is Observable going?"):** visible user
  unease with cloud-only notebooks, which the vendor answered with an open file
  format and a desktop app. → **Probe:** how quickly does the landing page make
  "your documents are files you own" legible?
- **Reported (industry commentary on agent tooling):** buyers in regulated
  settings reportedly refuse agents that cannot produce an audit trail
  "regardless of performance." Low confidence — this is vendor-adjacent
  commentary, not research.

## 4. Declared unknowables — the boundary of this run

Named so the brain does not generalize a biased public sample to the market.

- **Survivorship, both directions.** Only *public* deaths and *surviving*
  winners are visible. F2's "nobody died attempting commercial verified docs" is
  **not** evidence that the niche is safe — silent failures and stealth
  competitors are structurally invisible.
- **Self-report bias in post-mortems.** Distill's post-mortem was written by the
  team that burned out; burnout narratives systematically underweight the
  possibility that demand was thin. Founders rationalize toward market and
  timing and away from execution. Read F3 with that discount applied.
- **"No post-mortem found" ≠ cause known.** Gigantum's absorption has no stated
  cause and none was inferred.
- **Private economics.** Mintlify's $0 Pro tier says nothing about realized ACV
  — a free plan with a metered agent can out-earn a $250 subscription or can be
  a land-grab burning the Series B. Both look identical from outside.
- **Source conflict and staleness.** Competitor-authored pricing pages disagreed
  with vendors' own pages (ReadMe: $79/$349 vs the primary $0/$250/$3,000+).
  Primary was trusted; Hex and Deepnote remain third-party.
- **Correlation ≠ causation on funding.** Mintlify's $45M is evidence that
  investors believe docs will be consumed by agents. It is **not** evidence that
  any buyer will pay for verification. Norm AI's $120M is evidence about
  compliance agents, not about mechanically-derived lineage.
- **Regulation proves obligation, not purchase.** CRA and AI Act deadlines prove
  enterprises must produce audit trails. Nothing found indicates they will buy
  *this* to do it — and whether an auditor accepts mechanically-derived lineage
  in place of a maintained traceability matrix is **unknown and unanswerable by
  desk research**.
- **Watering-hole norms are mostly uncited.** Only Write the Docs' rules were
  read from a primary source. The rest is general knowledge at medium
  confidence and should be confirmed before `$community-engagement` posts.
- **Reachability of the compliance cohort is untested.** Its channels do not
  overlap with the other four cohorts' at all.

# Addendum — same day, after operator challenge

**Operator's challenge:** none of the five personas is a tech lead or product
manager, and "docs, but with verification" undersells the product — the thesis
is *provenance*: tracing requirements back to original sources, and proving you
are not hallucinating. Enterprises may pay for "all requirements trace back to
X, Y and Z," especially where development is contracted against requirements.

**Verdict: the challenge holds, with one correction to my own first pass and one
boundary the pitch must not cross.**

## A6 — Spec-driven development is a live, priced category I missed

The first pass did not search for it, which was a gap.

- **AWS Kiro** launched internationally **2026-05-07** as a ground-up replacement
  for Amazon Q Developer: a spec-driven IDE turning one prompt into a
  requirements doc in **EARS notation**, a design doc, and a
  dependency-sequenced task list, with **requirement-to-task traceability** and
  each test "directly traceable to a requirement in `specs/SPEC.md`." Priced
  **$20 / $40 / $100 / $200 per month**, metered on AI credits ($0.04 overage),
  Team tiers with SSO.
- **GitHub Spec Kit** is the MIT-licensed, agent-agnostic version: the spec
  **persists as editable repo artifacts rather than a throwaway chat plan**, and
  `tasks.md` keeps requirement-to-task traceability. **Free.**
- Plus BMAD-METHOD, OpenSpec, Augment Cosmos, `.cursor/rules`.

**Correction to the first pass.** Niche N3 recorded willingness to pay for
agent-output artifacts as "none found — unknown, not low." That is now wrong:
**$20–$200 per developer per month is the published price for requirements
traced through to code.** WTP is proven. The seat, however, is taken.

## A7 — The seam is *derived* vs *asserted*, and it is sharper than "verified docs"

Every alternative found — Kiro, Spec Kit, Jama, Polarion, codebeamer, DOORS, an
ADR, and **GSA's own RTM template, which ships as an `.xlsx` spreadsheet** —
records traceability as a **link a human or an agent asserted**. Not one of them
re-executes anything to prove the link still holds. DO-178C at DAL A demands
full bidirectional traceability across four levels and the literature calls
producing it "labor-intensive and susceptible to human error without appropriate
tools."

**So the operator is right that verification was the wrong headline — but not
because verification is secondary.** Provenance *without* verification is
precisely what the entire incumbent field already sells. Verification is the
mechanism that makes a provenance claim load-bearing. The defensible sentence is
**"the trace is derived and re-provable, not asserted,"** which holds against
every competitor found, simultaneously.

## A8 — The boundary the pitch must not cross (inference)

Hickory's provenance is mechanical from **executed inputs** to outputs. The link
from a **meeting note, client decision, or Jira ticket to a requirement** is
still an assertion, exactly like everyone else's — versioned and diffable in the
same document, which is better, but not derived.

The full "meeting notes → Jira → shipped product, every byte" story is therefore
a **roadmap claim, not a current capability claim.** Sold as the latter, the
first competent technical evaluator finds the join and the credibility of the
mechanical half goes with it. Sold accurately — "requirement → code → test →
output is derived; intent → requirement is asserted but versioned" — it is still
strictly better than anything in the alternatives set.

## A9 — The design constraint the graveyard already proved

Reported best practice for traceability is to **"capture evidence as a byproduct
of normal work rather than asking the team to do extra work."** That is the same
finding as F3: literate programming died of parallel maintenance burden. Any
trace requiring deliberate upkeep loses to the spreadsheet it replaced. This is
the strongest cross-confirmation in the entire recon — an old failure and a
current best practice pointing at the same constraint from opposite directions.

## A10 — New persona and niche

- **`personas/delivery-owner.md`** — the missing cohort. Unlike
  `compliance-provenance-enterprise`, **this one is reachable**: Hacker News,
  Jira, no procurement cycle. Carries an explicit **split hypothesis**
  (internal product org vs contract-bound delivery) with a recorded fork trigger,
  rather than being split a priori.
- **Niche N6** — derived traceability for requirements-bound delivery. It is the
  **self-serve front door to N2's value**: N6's buyer is reachable, N2's is not.
  If land-and-expand into compliance ever works, N6 is the land.

## Addendum unknowables

- **No public figure was found for what traceability costs a project today** —
  searched explicitly against DO-178C/IEC 62304, not found. The "labor-intensive"
  characterization is qualitative.
- **Kiro's prices are third-party**; kiro.dev/pricing was not fetched directly.
- **No tool vendor was found selling into fixed-price contract disputes.** The
  legal literature names RTMs as the evidence, but the market there appears to
  be lawyers and spreadsheets. Absence of a vendor is *not* evidence of absence
  of demand — it is the same ambiguity as F2, one layer over.
- **Whether an auditor or a contracting officer accepts a derived trace in place
  of a maintained matrix remains unknown**, and is now the highest-value
  unanswered question in the entire recon. It cannot be researched. It can be
  asked, in one conversation, of one person who has signed off on such a
  deliverable.
